# Versions — Variant C: "someone like you already did it"

One concept iterated v1→v8: an ordinary person with zero coding describes her
idea one afternoon and has a working app by dinner; the friend's idea could be
next, and starting takes one sentence. Counts are characters (code points).

- v1 (165): My neighbor has never written a line of code, but one afternoon she described her idea to an AI agent, and by dinner she was showing us her working app on her phone.
- v2 (225): My neighbor has never written a line of code, yet one afternoon she described her idea to an AI agent, and by dinner she was showing us her working app on her phone — your idea could be next, and all it takes is one sentence.
- v3 (203): My neighbor can't code at all, yet one afternoon she told an AI agent her idea for an app, and by dinner she was showing it to us on her phone — yours could be next, and starting takes just one sentence.
- v4 (227): My neighbor has never coded a thing, but one afternoon she described her idea to an AI agent, and by dinner she was passing her phone around showing us her working app — your idea could be next, and it starts with one sentence.
- v5 (225): My neighbor has never written a line of code, but one afternoon she told an AI agent her idea, and by dinner she was showing us the working app on her phone — yours could be next, and all it takes is telling it what you want.
- v6 (229): My neighbor has never written a line of code, but one afternoon she told an AI agent her idea, and by dinner she was passing her phone around showing us her working app — yours could be next, and it starts with a single sentence.
- v7 (225): My neighbor has never written a line of code, but one afternoon she told an AI agent her idea, and by dinner she was passing her phone around showing us the app it built — yours could be next, and it starts with one sentence.
- v8 (239, FINAL): My neighbor has never written a line of code, but one afternoon she told an AI agent her idea, and by dinner she was passing her phone around showing us the app it built — your idea could be next, and all it takes is one sentence to start.

Why v8 won: fullest use of the 240-char budget; "passing her phone around" is the most concrete proof image; "your idea could be next" addresses the friend directly; "one sentence to start" makes the ask feel tiny and low-risk. v2 was the runner-up but "showing us her working app" is weaker than the phone being passed around. v3/v5 lost on the weaker ending ("telling it what you want" is vague; "starting takes just one sentence" is less inviting than "all it takes is one sentence to start").
